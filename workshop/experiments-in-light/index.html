WORKSHOP / LIGHT & TRANSLUCENCE

Experiments in light

LEDs, lithophanes, translucent prints and attempts to make small illuminated objects look right.

Seeing what a print does with light

A lithophane looks like a rather odd piece of plastic until you put a light behind it. Translucent colours behave differently again. I've been experimenting with both, along with addressable LED strips and printed frames that hold the pieces together.

Part of the challenge is mechanical: keeping the panel removable, hiding the wiring and providing enough room for the lighting. The other part is optical: bright LED spots look poor when you want a softly illuminated picture.

What's on the bench

One lighting setup uses a 5 V SK6812 RGBW addressable LED strip with a GLEDOPTO ESP32 controller running WLED. Having a dedicated white channel is useful when the object needs to look like a lamp rather than a colour effect.

There's also a modular A5-sized optical lightbox design with a removable cassette and diffuser. The body was revised to deal with unsupported geometry in the initial design; that corrected design is not the same thing as having a finished physical lightbox.

Lithophanes and other pieces

The same approach can support ordinary lithophanes, translucent decorative panels and replaceable backlit inserts. I want to keep the best of those as separate little records when I have photographs of the actual prints and lighting conditions.

Images wanted: the LED strip and controller on the bench; a lithophane with the light off and on; and a real lightbox or insert test. Until then this page uses a clearly labelled layer diagram.
