WORKSHOP / REUSED HARDWARE

Old laptop, new job

An older Lenovo, a reused drive and Linux Mint became a small local Hub—and acquired a much more dramatic cabinet than the hardware deserved.

One more job for an old laptop

I had an older Lenovo laptop and a drive that could be reused, so I gave the hardware another job rather than buying a new server. It became a local place for small household and workshop applications: something that could stay on, hold a little state and run independently of whichever computer I was using.

The useful experiments

The Hub became a base for a household console, inbox and calendar experiments, workshop intake and other small tools. Pi-hole was one of the practical detours: a local network filtering experiment that taught me more about DNS, maintenance and how quickly a small useful service can become something people expect to keep working.

Not every experiment has to become permanent. The useful part is learning which jobs belong on the machine, which deserve proper recovery notes and which should remain easy to remove.

Behind the Reliquary

The machine now lives behind the Arcane Hub Reliquary Screen. That is a grand name for a decorated cabinet around a recycled laptop, but it captures the mixture rather well: ordinary hardware doing useful work behind an increasingly elaborate physical object.

The public story stops there. Network addresses, credentials, private household records and access details do not belong in the gallery.
